What changed this quarter

Four laboratory papers moved formaldehyde-free binders closer to panel conditions: a cysteine-lignin plywood glue, a starch–citric acid MDF binder, a lignin–tannin NIPU curing at 130 °C and a veneer-faced spent-grain particleboard. None ran a UF control. No verified industrial bio-binder or bio-acrylic milestone appeared before the cutoff.

Tags: BIO-DERIVED FOSSIL-TYPICAL ORIGIN ND INORGANIC · Strategy A = renewable version of the same chemistry, B = different chemistry · ND = not disclosed

1

Cysteine-grafted lignin bonds plywood without formaldehyde

Change. Bagasse lignin grafted with cysteine self-cures in the press as a 20 wt% aqueous glue. Two-layer poplar plywood reached 4.0 MPa dry [1].

Implication. At 1.96 mPa·s it can be sprayed. Its ‘beats commercial resin’ claim uses PF diluted to 20 % solids, not UF or PF as supplied [1].

Gap. Stable wet strength needed hotter pressing (190 °C, 8 min). Cysteine recovery and cost remain unresolved [1].

2

Citric acid and urea upgrade starch binder for MDF

Change. Starch with sorbitol, glycerol, citric acid and urea bonded 10 mm lab MDF at 10 % dry resin: IB 0.59–0.86 MPa, 2 h swelling 22.74–24.54 % [2].

Implication. An aldehyde-free MDF route on commodity inputs; urea (8.2 % of solids, computed) makes it hybrid. Press: 180 °C, 7 min (≈42 s/mm, computed) [2].

Gap. No UF control, rheology, pot life or emission test [2].

3

Lignin–tannin NIPU cures birch plywood at 130 °C

Change. Diamine-grafted lignin and oxidised, methoxycarbonylated tannin form urethane links without isocyanate. At 130 °C, 10 min: 1.89 MPa dry, 0.85 MPa wet (EN 314-1) [3].

Implication. Brings NIPU cure into plywood press range; the authors' control NIPUs peaked near 180–200 °C [3].

Gap. Wet strength is below 1.0 MPa and passes only via the ≥40 % wood-failure rule. HMDA is fossil-typical; spread 300 g/m² [3].

4

Veneer faces lift spent-grain board to P2 bending

Change. Spent grain bonded with boric-acid-modified soy protein missed EN 312 bending minimums. With 0.64 mm veneer faces, boards met P2 and ANSI H1 flexural minimums [4].

Implication. A residue board with no added formaldehyde, but at about 1000 kg/m³ and a 25 min press at 140 °C [4].

Gap. Internal bond untested on faced boards; no UF control or emission data [4].

Watchlist

  • Crosslinker A hyperbranched imine adhesive gave a wood-particle composite (pressed at 120 °C) that kept 100 % of its properties over three reprocessing cycles. Abstract only; monomer origin ND [5].
  • Crosslinker Soybean meal with PAE, melamine-urea-glyoxal and blocked polyurethane pressed plywood at 105 °C in 4 min (wet shear 0.97 MPa). All three crosslinkers are fossil-typical, so it is hybrid. Abstract only [6].
  • Acrylic acid No verified renewable acrylic-acid supply or scale milestone between 1 Sep and 14 Oct; earlier pilots are background only.
  • Regulation Comments on US EPA's Section 610 review of the TSCA Title VI composite-wood formaldehyde rule closed on 13 Oct; outcome pending [7].

Three follow-up questions

  1. MID Does cysteine-grafted lignin keep its wet strength at ≤130 °C on 3-ply panels, against UF and undiluted PF at equal spread?
  2. MIM What press factor does the starch–citric acid binder need on an MDF line, against UF at equal resin load and density?
  3. MTE Can a bio-based diamine replace HMDA in the lignin–tannin NIPU and keep the 130 °C cure?
